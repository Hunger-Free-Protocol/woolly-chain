The Core Idea
A Blockchain That Feeds People
Unlike Proof of Work (which wastes energy) or Proof of Stake (which locks up capital), Proof of Nourishment derives chain security from the verified productive activity of growing food.

Sustainability is the primary consensus driver

The more sustainably you farm, the more influence you have over the chain. This is the first blockchain where doing good for the planet directly increases your consensus power.

Zero transaction fees

Farming IS the work. No gas fees, ever. Network costs are funded by a 5% produce settlement fee and 1% carbon credit issuance fee — built into the economy, not taxing every action.

Four layers of Sybil resistance

Physical sensors with centimeter GPS accuracy. Real land and capital that's expensive to fake. Multi-cycle track records taking 6–12 months to build. And cryptographic cross-validation from three or more neighboring farms.

The Endgame
Three Paths to Free Food
Free food is not charity. It is a contribution-based economic model. Everyone contributes something — capital, land, or labor — and receives food in return.
PATH 01

Invest Capital

Invest above the minimum threshold and receive a lifetime food subscription plus revenue from farm operations. The more you invest, the higher your tier.

Min $5,000 → 50 kg/month for life + revenue share
PATH 02

Contribute Land

Dedicate agricultural land to the protocol. You receive food proportional to your land area plus 25% of all revenue the farm generates.

Min 500 sq ft → proportional food + 25% revenue
PATH 03

Work the Farm

Contribute your labor. A portion of your wages converts to farm equity over time. After the minimum commitment period, your food subscription becomes free.

Min 6 months → wages + equity → free food for life

Operational Heritage
Built on Six Years of Real Farming
Woolly Protocol didn't start in a whitepaper. It started in 80,000 sq ft of hydroponic farms across Indian agroecological zones.

From 2016 to 2022, Woolly Farms operated controlled environment agriculture (CEA) facilities, testing the five-persona stakeholder model across 200+ crop cycles. The Woolly Brain — an 11-microservice farm management system — directly informed the design of today's autonomous agent architecture.

Post-harvest losses were reduced from 14.2% to 4.8% through logistics coordination. Insurance partners confirmed that telemetry fused with satellite data meets the threshold for parametric insurance products. Every design decision in the protocol traces back to something learned on the ground.
